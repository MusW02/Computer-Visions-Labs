DISCUSSION

1. WHICH MODEL PERFORMS BEST FOR REAL-TIME DETECTION?
-----------------------------------------------------
Winner: SSD (Single Shot MultiBox Detector) with MobileNet backbone.

Reasoning:
[cite_start]As defined in the lab manual, SSD is designed specifically to predict bounding boxes and class probabilities in a "single forward pass," making it highly efficient[cite: 122, 124]. The specific version used (MobileNet backbone) is optimized for speed, allowing it to achieve the highest FPS during testing compared to the heavier YOLOv3 and RetinaNet architectures. It eliminates the complex region proposal stages found in two-stage detectors, ensuring fluid video feedback.


2. WHICH MODEL PERFORMS BEST FOR SMALL OBJECT RECOGNITION?
--------------------------------------------------------
Winner: RetinaNet.

Reasoning:
[cite_start]RetinaNet utilizes a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) as its backbone[cite: 140]. This structure extracts features at multiple resolutions, allowing the model to detect small objects that often disappear in the lower-resolution feature maps of faster models like SSD. [cite_start]Additionally, RetinaNet was introduced with "Focal Loss" specifically to solve the problem of class imbalance, focusing the training on hard-to-classify examples (often small objects) rather than easy background areas[cite: 138, 141]. 
(Note: YOLOv3 also performed well in this category compared to older versions due to its multi-scale prediction, but RetinaNet is theoretically specialized for this task).


3. WHICH MODEL PERFORMS BEST FOR RESOURCE-LIMITED SYSTEMS (CPU-ONLY)?
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Winner: SSD (MobileNet).

Reasoning:
Resource-limited systems (such as standard laptops, Raspberry Pis, or mobile phones) require models with low memory footprints and low computational costs.
- Size: The SSD MobileNet model size is approximately 20 MB, significantly smaller than YOLOv3 (~237 MB) and RetinaNet (~145 MB).
- Efficiency: It uses depthwise separable convolutions to reduce the number of parameters.
[cite_start]This makes SSD the only viable option among the three for systems relying solely on a CPU for inference[cite: 127].
